Does Your Website Say 'Not Secure'? Customers Leave Immediately — And SSL Certificates Are Getting Much Shorter

Expired SSL certificates cost you customers and hurt rankings. With certs shortening to 47 days, automatic SSL management is becoming essential.

You open a website in your browser and see this in the address bar: 🔴 “Your connection is not private”

What do you do? Most likely you hit the back button.

That’s exactly what your customers do too. And you may not even notice — because an expired SSL certificate usually doesn’t notify the website owner.

What Is an SSL Certificate and Why Does It Matter?#

An SSL certificate (Secure Sockets Layer) is a digital document that encrypts the connection between your website and visitors. The padlock symbol in the address bar and “https://” confirm its presence.

SSL has two core functions:

1. Security: Encrypts data flowing between the visitor’s browser and your server. A patient filling out a contact form, a customer entering payment details — this data travels encrypted.

2. Trust: When a visitor sees the padlock, they know the site is legitimate. The “not secure” warning does the opposite.

The True Cost of an Expired SSL Certificate#

Many businesses treat SSL certificates as “set it and forget it.” They buy a one- or two-year certificate, it expires, they don’t notice — sometimes for weeks.

What happens during that time:

Customer loss: The vast majority of visitors who encounter a “not secure” warning leave immediately. Contact forms, appointment requests, online orders — none of it gets completed.

Google ranking drop: Google pushes sites without SSL or with expired certificates down in search results. Organic traffic built up over months can erode quickly.

Reputation damage: A customer who sees “not secure” carries a lasting negative impression of that business.

The Big Change Coming: SSL Certificates Dropping to 47 Days#

Currently SSL certificates are valid for 90 days or one year. That is changing.

The CA/Browser Forum — the body that brings together certificate authorities and major browsers — has passed a new requirement: in the coming years, SSL certificate validity will drop to 47 days. Apple, Google, and Mozilla all support this decision.

What does this mean? If you currently renew once a year, you will soon need to renew 7–8 times per year. Manual tracking becomes nearly impossible.

Why was this decided? Shorter validity periods limit the impact of compromised certificates and push the industry toward automation — which is actually a positive development.

The Solution: Automatic SSL Management with Cloudflare#

When Cloudflare takes over your website’s DNS, it manages your SSL certificate automatically. It renews before expiry without you needing to track anything. When validity drops to 47 days, it makes no difference — everything is handled in the background.

This feature is available even on the free Cloudflare plan.

Beyond SSL: WAF and DDoS Protection#

SSL is just the beginning. When you switch to Cloudflare, you also get:

WAF (Web Application Firewall): Protects your website from SQL injection, XSS attacks, and bot traffic. Malicious requests are blocked before they reach your website.

DDoS Protection: Attack traffic aimed at taking your website down is automatically detected and filtered.

Analytics: How many visitors came, from where, how many attacks were blocked — monthly report.

No Hosting Migration Needed#

The most common question: “Do I need to move my website?”

No. Cloudflare only operates at the DNS level. Your website stays where it is — Ionos, Strato, wherever. We simply move your domain’s DNS records to Cloudflare. Setup takes 1–2 hours, and your visitors notice nothing.
