Cisco Catalyst 3560 IOS Upgrade: Two Methods and Verification

Upgrading IOS on a Catalyst 3560 is not complicated, but the details matter: which file format you download, how much space is left in flash, and whether the boot variable points to the right image. A mistake in any of these leaves you with a switch that boots into the boot loader instead of IOS. There are two ways to do it: Method A: archive download-sw with a .tar file. The switch extracts the image, the web device manager files and sets the boot variable itself. Method B: manual upgrade with a .bin file. You copy the image, verify it, set boot system and reload. Cisco Catalyst 3560 Password Recovery: Two Methods, Step by Step

You inherit a switch, the documentation is gone, and nobody knows the enable password. Or a lab switch comes back from storage and the console asks for a password you never set. On a Cisco Catalyst 3560 you can recover from both without losing the configuration, as long as you have physical access and a console cable. This guide covers the two situations you will meet in practice: Method 1: keep the configuration. The switch boots without loading its startup configuration, you reset the passwords, and you put the original configuration back. Method 2: password recovery is disabled. Someone configured no service password-recovery. You can still get the switch back, but only by resetting it to factory defaults.
